Opportunities

The Archives, Memory, and Preservation Lab (AMPL) is a digital humanities lab at UCSB's Center for Latin American and Iberian Research. We work with archives, libraries, and communities in Latin America and the United States to preserve historical materials and make them available to the people who need them. Undergraduates take part in every stage of this work, and we recruit at all levels of experience.

Build
We design, build, and test low-cost scanners, and the software that runs them, so that small archives and community organizations can digitize their own collections.
Digitize
We photograph manuscripts, newspapers, photographs, and personal papers to archival standards, in the lab and on site.
Catalog
We describe what we digitize (who produced each document, when, and what it contains) so that researchers and communities can find it.
Read
We train students to read sixteenth- and seventeenth-century Spanish handwriting, and to ask research questions of what they read.
Share
We make the results accessible within and beyond the university: we publish archives on Zasqua, build visual narratives with Telar, and prepare editions of sources for classrooms and the public.

For graduates

Interested in archives, memory, or historical preservation? Work with a collection you'd like to help protect, or share with new audiences? Get in touch! We're always looking for opportunities to collaborate.

For undergraduates

AMPL is always looking for interested and motivated undergraduates to join the team. Undergraduates have the opportunity to be involved in all aspects of our research, including archival digitization and description, the development and testing of tools and methods, the production of public-facing narratives, and even helping formulate new research questions. Research assistants are expected to work approximately 3–6 hours per week in the lab (or remotely, depending on the project), including a weekly lab meeting, and can receive course credit. For some projects we require a two-quarter commitment. Roles are also listed on the URCA Undergraduate Research Directory.
